## Two models

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Pay as you go" icon="gauge-simple">
    Billed per minute of conversation. No seats, no platform minimum, no annual commitment. You pay for the calls you actually take.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Enterprise" icon="building-columns">
    For high-volume or regulated operations. Reserved capacity, an SLA, controlled deployment, residency options, and a named solutions team.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## The rates

Pay as you go is metered per unit of work, at the rates the console charges today: **$0.09 per connected agent minute**, plus the per-product rates for everything that is not a call.

<Card title="Rate card" icon="tag" href="/pricing">
  Every product, every unit, and what a month costs at your volume.
</Card>

Enterprise is quoted against your committed minutes rather than listed. Volume bands and the enterprise floor are set in the agreement, not published.

## What a minute includes

A minute is a minute of agent conversation on a connected call, rounded up. Recognition, the agent's reasoning, synthesis, actions against your systems, and the transcript and summary afterwards are all part of it.

A live call is metered by connected time rather than by turn, because the line is held open whether or not anyone is speaking.

## What is quoted separately

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Telephony and numbers" icon="phone">
    Number rental and call carriage are carrier costs, quoted by region and number type and passed through at cost. See [Phone numbers](/numbers).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Implementation" icon="wrench">
    Building the first workflow against your systems, where you want that done for you rather than by your own team.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The systems the agent acts in" icon="plug">
    Your CRM, ticketing, and scheduling licences remain yours. See [Integrations](/integrations).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What enterprise adds

- **Reserved capacity** against your peak concurrency, rather than best effort.
- **A service level**, with the response commitments written down.
- **Residency options**, including in-country hosted deployment — see [Deployment options](/deployment).
- **SSO and SCIM**, so access follows your directory.
- **Audit-log streaming** into your own SIEM.
- **Security documentation** for procurement review.
- **A named solutions team** rather than a shared queue.

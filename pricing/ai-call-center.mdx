Agents that answer your lines in Saudi Arabic, do what the caller asked in your systems, and hand over to a person when they should.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="SAR 0.15 per minute" icon="phone">
    Every minute an agent spends on a call. Nothing else is metered.
  </Card>
  <Card title="\$0.04 per minute" icon="dollar-sign">
    The same price at the peg of SAR 3.75 = \$1.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

That one number covers the whole call: understanding Najdi, Gulf, Egyptian or Modern Standard Arabic, working out what the caller wants, doing it in your systems, speaking the reply, and writing the transcript and summary afterwards.

No seat fee, no platform fee, no per-agent charge, nothing extra for dialects, nothing extra for transcripts.

## What a month costs

| Calls a month | Average call | Minutes | Riyals | Dollars |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 500 | 3 minutes | 1,500 | **SAR 225** | \$60 |
| 2,000 | 4 minutes | 8,000 | **SAR 1,200** | \$320 |
| 10,000 | 4 minutes | 40,000 | **SAR 6,000** | \$1,600 |
| 50,000 | 5 minutes | 250,000 | **SAR 37,500** | \$10,000 |

<Tip>
  A receptionist in Saudi Arabia costs SAR 2,000 to 5,000 a month and works eight hours a day, five days a week. The first row is a line answered every hour of every day for less than a tenth of one salary.
</Tip>

Past roughly 750,000 minutes a year, the on-premise licence is cheaper than the cloud as well as more private.

## Not included

**Telephony** — the number and the call carriage are the phone company's charges, passed through at cost. See [Phone numbers](/numbers).

**Setting up the first workflow**, if you want it done for you: a one-off SAR 37,500 / \$10,000.

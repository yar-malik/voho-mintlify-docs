Joins the systems a facilities team runs between — Archibus, SAP, BMS, ticketing — so a reported fault becomes a work order, an assignment and a closed loop without anyone retyping it.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="SAR 0.04 per work order" icon="building">
    A fault resolved into an asset, a trade, a priority and an SLA.
  </Card>
  <Card title="\$0.01 per work order" icon="dollar-sign">
    The same price at the peg of SAR 3.75 = \$1.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
  If tenants report faults by **phone**, the call is priced as [AI Call Center](/pricing/ai-call-center) — SAR 0.15 / \$0.04 a minute — and the work order it produces is included in that. The per-work-order rate is for faults arriving by app, form or API.
</Note>

## Where it runs

<Tabs>

  </Tab>

  <Tab title="In-Kingdom hosted">
    **Quoted against your deployment**, not sold from a price list — a dedicated deployment operated for you and sized to your concurrency.

    Where processing physically happens is the entire point of this tier, so it is settled during scoping and written into the contract rather than asserted on a pricing page. If in-country processing is a requirement you have to evidence to a regulator, **On-premise** answers it without qualification.

| Work orders a month | Riyals | Dollars |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000 | **SAR 38** | \$10 |
| 10,000 | **SAR 375** | \$100 |

Riyal totals are at the peg; the per-work-order figure above is rounded up to the nearest halala.

## Not included

**Writing the ticket into Archibus or SAP.** Voho produces the work order; posting it into your system of record is an integration, connected once. A one-off SAR 37,500 / \$10,000 if you want it done for you. See [Integrations](/integrations).

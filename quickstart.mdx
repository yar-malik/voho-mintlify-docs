This walks through standing up one agent on one phone line. Plan on a working prototype the same day and a tuned production line over the following week.

## Before you start

You need:

- A Voho account with at least one agent slot provisioned. Contact your Voho representative if you are not sure.
- A phone number to route to the agent, or a test number issued by Voho.
- Somewhere for the agent to send its results — a ticketing system, CRM, or an endpoint of your own.

## Get started

<Steps>
  <Step title="Describe the job">
    Write down what the agent answers, in plain language: who calls this line, what they usually want, and what counts as a resolved call. This becomes the agent's instructions, and it matters more to call quality than any other setting.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set dialect and voice">
    Choose the dialect the agent replies in and the voice that renders it. Najdi Arabic is the default for Saudi deployments. See [Voices and languages](/voices).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect your systems">
    Give the agent the actions it is allowed to take — creating a ticket, looking up an order, updating a record. An agent with no actions can only talk; the actions are what make the call useful.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Call it yourself">
    Ring the line and try the three most common requests, then try one deliberately awkward one: a caller who changes their mind, or who mixes Arabic and English mid-sentence. Listen for whether the agent stays in the right dialect.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and adjust">
    Read the transcripts and summaries from your test calls. Most early problems are instruction problems, not model problems — tighten the wording and call again.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Going to production

Before pointing real traffic at the line:

- **Test with real accents.** Recruit callers who speak the way your customers actually speak, not colleagues reading a script.
- **Decide the handoff.** Every line needs a path to a human. Define what triggers it and where the call lands.
- **Watch the first week.** Read transcripts daily at first. The failure patterns show up quickly and are usually cheap to fix.

An on-premise deployment is installed with you rather than handed over as a download. A named solutions engineer works alongside your team, and the first workflow is normally live within a month.

Voho ships as container images, in three deployment forms — Docker on a single GPU server, Kubernetes for a cluster, or one self-contained image where the platform requires it — with a signed licence file the deployment validates locally.

<Note>
  The registry, image references and configuration values are specific to the release you will run and are provided with the runbook for your deployment.

## Choose the deployment form

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Docker">
    One GPU server, Docker with NVIDIA GPU support. The usual starting point — a single node is a production configuration, not a trial one. Choose this unless you have a reason not to.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Kubernetes">
    A cluster. Choose this when you are scaling past one node, or when your estate standardises on Kubernetes and a lone Docker host would be the exception nobody patches.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Single container">
    One self-contained image, for platforms that only accept a single-container deployment. Choose this when your hosting platform forces it, not by preference.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## The path

<Steps>
  <Step title="Scoping" icon="clipboard-list">
    One call covering capacity, environment, obligations and operations. Bring the numbers from [Requirements](/on-prem/requirements) — peak concurrent calls above all.

    **Out of it:** a sizing for your traffic, a deployment shape, and the list of what each side provides.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Environment pre-flight" icon="server">
    Your team provisions hosts, GPUs, storage and network paths, and creates the service accounts the agent will act through. Voho reviews the environment against the sizing before anything is installed.

    **Out of it:** an environment that passes review, so install day is install day.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Install" icon="download">
    Voho and your team deploy together, in your environment, against your runbook: pull the images, place the signed licence file, start the deployment, and confirm the GPU is addressed. Nothing is installed that your team has not seen installed.

    **Out of it:** a running deployment, health-checked, with the local dashboard showing usage and system health.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Connect telephony" icon="phone">
    Point a test number at the deployment over your existing trunk. Audio is 8 kHz mulaw, so there is no transcoding tier to build.

    **Out of it:** a real call, answered, in your building.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Build the first workflow" icon="bolt">
    Configure the agent's instructions and wire the actions it needs into your systems. Start with one workflow that matters and finishes — a status enquiry, a ticket being raised — not a broad assistant.

    See [Instructions](/instructions) and [Actions](/actions).

    **Out of it:** an agent that completes a task end to end, including its handover path.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Test before traffic" icon="vial">
    Run it against real recordings and awkward cases: heavy dialect, code-switching mid-sentence, callers who interrupt, reference numbers read back digit by digit. See [Testing](/testing).

    **Out of it:** a pass criterion agreed before the first customer hears it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Pilot" icon="chart-line">
    Route a slice of live traffic. Watch time to first audio, handover rate and action failures. Fix what the transcripts show rather than what the demo suggested.

    **Out of it:** evidence from your own callers.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Cutover and handover" icon="key">
    Widen traffic, and move first-line operations to your team with the runbooks, dashboards and escalation path in place.

    **Out of it:** a deployment your team runs. See [Operations](/on-prem/operations).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Prepare before day one

Every item here is something only your organisation can produce, and each one blocks the step it belongs to.

- **Peak concurrent calls**, from the busiest hour of the last year.
- **A GPU host** provisioned to the sizing — 16–24 GB VRAM or better, 8 cores, 32 GB RAM, 50–100 GB disk — on Ubuntu or Debian, with NVIDIA drivers and Docker GPU support in place.
- **Network paths** opened: trunk to call handling, action layer to each target system.
- **Service accounts** on each system the agent will act in, scoped to only what its actions need.
- **Identity provider** ready for SSO, with your role definitions decided.
- **Retention policy** per data type — audio, transcripts, summaries — agreed with your legal and data protection people.
- **A test number** you can route freely without touching production traffic.
- **A firewall decision** on the periodic usage sync, or an agreed offline path if you are deploying with zero egress.
- **The first workflow chosen**, with the person who owns that process available.

## Who needs to be in the room

| Role | Needed for |
| --- | --- |
| Infrastructure / platform | Hosts, GPUs, storage, network |
| Telephony | Trunk configuration and test numbering |
| Security | Boundary review, service accounts, audit routing |
| Identity | SSO and SCIM |
| Application owners | APIs and service accounts on the target systems |
| Process owner | What the first workflow must actually do |
| Legal / data protection | Retention, residency and caller notification |

<Tip>
  The two things that most often slow a deployment down are service accounts on target systems and a firewall change nobody raised a ticket for. Start both in week one.
</Tip>
